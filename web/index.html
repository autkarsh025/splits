          How it works

          Equivalent times. Every time becomes a VDOT score using the Daniels and Gilbert equations: the oxygen cost of your pace divided by the share of your aerobic maximum you can hold for that long. Running the equations backwards gives equivalent times and training paces.

          Rankings. Percentiles come from the full spread of finish times, not averages. A shorter race is ranked against the marathon field through its marathon equivalent. College rankings use each athlete’s best time in the event.

          Race plans. The goal leans on your race closest in distance, then adds time for racing far beyond your longest race, for hills (about 1% rolling, 3% hilly) and for heat. Checkpoints follow a slightly faster second half from 10K up.

          Limits. Marathon fields are self-selected, so beating 60% of them is better than beating 60% of people. Equivalent times assume you trained for the distance.
