        How it works

        Equivalent times. Every time is converted to VDOT with the Daniels and Gilbert equations: the oxygen cost of running at your pace divided by the share of VO2max you can hold for that long. Inverting the same equations gives equivalent times at other distances and the training paces.

        Rankings. Percentiles are read from the full distribution of finish times, not from averages. A shorter race is ranked against the marathon field through its marathon equivalent. College rankings use each athlete’s best time in that event.

        Race plans. The goal leans on your race closest in distance, then adds time for racing far beyond your longest race, for hills (about 1% rolling, 3% hilly) and for heat. Checkpoints follow a slightly faster second half from 10K up.

        Limits. Marathon fields are self-selected, so beating 60% of them is better than beating 60% of all people. Equivalents assume you trained for the distance.
